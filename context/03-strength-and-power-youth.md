# Strength and Power for Youth Athletes

## Evidence Position (important for the AI to state when asked)

Supervised, technique-led resistance training is **safe and beneficial for children**
and does not stunt growth — this myth is explicitly rejected by NSCA, ACSM and the
international consensus statement. Injury rates in supervised youth resistance
programs are lower than in the team sports themselves. The risks come from
unsupervised maximal lifting and poor technique, not from resistance training itself.

## Progression Ladder (competency-gated, not age-gated)

1. **Movement competency (entry level, any age from ~6–7):** bodyweight squat,
   lunge, hinge pattern, push-up, inverted row, crawling, hanging, single-leg balance.
2. **Loaded technique (typically 9–12+):** goblet squats, light dumbbell work,
   medicine-ball throws, band rows. Loads that allow 10–15 crisp reps.
3. **Structured resistance (post technical mastery, typically 13+):** barbell
   variations with qualified supervision; 2–3 sessions/week; 6–12 reps at loads
   leaving 2–3 reps in reserve. No true 1RM testing before late adolescence.
4. **Power development (post-PHV):** jump squats, loaded carries, sled pushes,
   introductory weightlifting derivatives (hang pull, hang power clean) if coached
   by an accredited coach.

## Plyometrics by Stage

- **Pre-PHV:** low-intensity only — skipping, hopping games, low bounds, line jumps.
  These double as bone-loading stimulus, which is valuable in this window.
- **Circa-PHV:** reduce impact volume during fastest growth; keep landing mechanics
  drills (stick landings, drop-to-stick from low box ≤30 cm).
- **Post-PHV:** progressive bounding, hurdle jumps, depth jumps last (≥2 years of
  consistent strength training first). Ground contacts per session: 60–100.

## Sample Templates

### Pre-PHV "Movement Strength" (20–25 min, attach to skills session)
Animal crawls 3×10 m · bodyweight squats 3×10 · push-up ladder · single-leg
balance with ball catch 3×20 s/leg · broad jump with stick landing 3×4 · hang from
bar 3× max comfortable.

### Post-PHV Team-Sport Strength (45 min, 2×/week)
A1 Goblet/back squat 3×8 · A2 Box jump 3×4 · B1 Romanian deadlift 3×8 ·
B2 Push-up/bench progression 3×8–10 · C1 Inverted row/chin-up 3×6–10 ·
C2 Nordic hamstring lowers 3×4–6 · D Loaded carry 3×30 m · Trunk circuit 6 min.

Nordics and Copenhagen adductor work are the two highest-value injury-prevention
exercises for football and rugby adolescents — include at least one weekly.

## Sex-Specific Notes (post-PHV only)

- **Females:** strength training is the single best ACL-risk modifier; prioritise
  posterior chain (RDLs, Nordics), single-leg strength, and landing mechanics.
  Same loading principles as males — do not program "lighter by default".
- **Males:** rapid post-PHV strength gains can outpace tendon adaptation; hold
  technique standards firmly and progress load ≤10% per week.

## Rugby-Specific Caveat

Mass gain for rugby should only be pursued post-PHV, food-first, and never via
supplements at junior level (most governing bodies prohibit supplement promotion
to minors; see World Rugby keep-rugby-clean guidance in ytkb-006).
