# Injury Prevention, Growth-Related Risk and Recovery

## Default Warm-Up Protocols (embed in every generated program)

- **Football:** FIFA 11+ Kids (ages 7–13) or FIFA 11+ (14+). ~15–20 min: running
  drills, strength/plyometric/balance block, sport-specific running. Evidence:
  ~40–50% injury reduction when done 2+ times weekly.
- **Rugby:** Activate (age-banded versions). Evidence: up to 72% concussion
  reduction at high compliance in school trials.
- These double as the speed/strength micro-dose for time-poor community teams.

## Growth-Related Conditions (the AI should recognise and adapt, not diagnose)

| Condition | Site | Typical window | Program adaptation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Osgood-Schlatter | Tibial tuberosity (knee) | Circa-PHV | Reduce jump/sprint volume, keep pain ≤3/10, strength via low-impact |
| Sever's disease | Heel | 8–12 | Reduce running on hard ground, calf flexibility/strength, heel pain monitoring |
| Spondylolysis risk | Lumbar | Adolescent, extension-loaded | Avoid loaded lumbar hyperextension; refer if persistent back pain |

Persistent pain >2 weeks, night pain, or pain altering movement → recommend
qualified medical review. The AI never provides diagnosis or return-to-play
clearance.

## ACL Risk — Post-PHV Females (and all cutting-sport athletes)

- Risk rises sharply post-PHV in female athletes (2–8× male rates in cutting sports).
- Mitigation: year-round neuromuscular training (FIFA 11+/Activate), posterior-chain
  and single-leg strength, deceleration and landing technique ("land like a ninja":
  quiet, knee over toe, hip back), exposure to cutting at progressive speeds.

## Concussion (both sports)

- Recognise and Remove. No same-day return at junior level under any circumstances.
- Graduated return-to-play: symptom-free progression through stages over a minimum
  period per the relevant governing body's current protocol (check World Rugby /
  national football association links — protocols are updated frequently).
- Return-to-learn (school) precedes return-to-sport.

## Load Management Rules of Thumb

- Weekly organised sport hours ≤ age in years.
- Avoid week-to-week load spikes >20–30% (sessions × RPE as the simple metric).
- 1–2 rest days weekly; one full off-season block annually; multi-sport encouraged
  to age 15–16.

## Recovery Essentials (age-appropriate, low-tech)

- **Sleep is the number-one recovery tool:** 9–11 h (children), 8–10 h (teens).
- **Food-first nutrition:** carbohydrate + protein within ~1–2 h post-session;
  water as default hydration; no supplements for minors.
- **Active recovery:** light games, swimming, walking the day after matches.
- Cold baths, compression, massage guns etc. are optional extras at youth level —
  never let them displace sleep, food and load management in generated advice.

## Mental and Social Wellbeing Guardrails for Generated Programs

- Enjoyment is the strongest predictor of retention — every junior program should
  contain visible fun, not just disguised conditioning.
- No public weigh-ins, body-composition targets, or appearance-based feedback for
  minors under any circumstances.
- Encourage athlete voice: simple post-session questions ("what felt good, what
  was hard?") build self-regulation.
