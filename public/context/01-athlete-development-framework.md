# Athlete Development Framework

## 1. Development Stages

Use these stages to set session intent, ratios of training-to-competition, and
acceptable intensity. Ages are guides; maturation status overrides.

### Foundation / FUNdamentals (≈5–8 years, pre-PHV)
- **Intent:** fundamental movement skills (FMS) — running, jumping, hopping, skipping,
  throwing, catching, kicking, balancing, dodging.
- **Format:** games-based, maximum variety, no formal periodisation. Every session
  should feel like play.
- **Speed note:** this window is excellent for developing movement frequency and
  coordination — short bursts (<5 s), full recovery, tag games and relays.
- **Avoid:** lining kids up for laps, isolated fitness work, early single-sport
  specialisation.

### Learn to Train (≈9–12 years, pre-PHV)
- **Intent:** convert FMS into sport skills; golden age of motor learning.
- **Speed and agility:** highest trainability of skill-based speed; prioritise
  acceleration mechanics, change of direction technique, reactive games.
- **Strength:** bodyweight mastery — squats, lunges, push-ups, crawling patterns,
  hanging. Introduce light external load only once technique is consistent.
- **Ratio:** roughly 70% training / 30% competition.

### Circa-PHV / Growth Spurt (≈11–14 girls, ≈13–15 boys)
- **Intent:** maintain and re-coach coordination as limbs lengthen rapidly
  ("adolescent awkwardness"); aerobic trainability peaks around PHV.
- **Watch for:** temporary drops in agility and coordination, growth-related
  overuse complaints (Osgood-Schlatter, Sever's — see ytkb-007).
- **Adjust:** reduce high-impact plyometric volume during the most rapid growth;
  increase mobility and movement re-patterning.

### Post-PHV / Train to Train → Train to Compete (≈14–18)
- **Intent:** strength and power become highly trainable (especially boys, driven by
  hormonal changes); structured periodisation appropriate; position-specific work.
- **Strength:** progressive resistance training with qualified supervision is safe and
  strongly recommended (see ytkb-003).
- **Females post-PHV:** prioritise neuromuscular knee-control programs (ACL injury
  risk rises 2–8× relative to males in cutting sports). Embed Activate / FIFA 11+
  every session.

## 2. Estimating Maturation Without Lab Testing

If the AI system has access to athlete height history, flag PHV onset as the period
of fastest height gain (>7 cm/year is a strong indicator). Practical proxies:
- Rapid shoe/clothing size changes reported by parents.
- Maturity offset equations (Mirwald) need sitting height — only use if collected.
- When unknown: girls 11–14 and boys 13–15 should be treated as potentially circa-PHV.

## 3. Trainability Windows (practical interpretation)

All qualities are trainable at all ages; the windows indicate where return on
training time is highest, not exclusivity.

| Quality | Pre-PHV | Circa-PHV | Post-PHV |
|---|---|---|---|
| Movement skill / coordination | ★★★ | ★★ (re-coach) | ★★ |
| Speed (neural/frequency) | ★★★ | ★★ | ★★★ (force-based) |
| Aerobic endurance | ★★ | ★★★ | ★★★ |
| Strength | ★★ (bodyweight, technique) | ★★ | ★★★ |
| Power/plyometrics | ★ (low-level only) | ★ (reduce volume) | ★★★ |

## 4. Weekly Load Guidance

- Total organised sport hours per week should not exceed the athlete's age in years
  (widely used paediatric guideline).
- Minimum 1–2 full rest days per week; minimum 2–3 months per year away from any
  single sport.
- Encourage sport sampling until at least 15–16 for team-sport athletes; late
  specialisation correlates with lower injury rates and longer careers.
