# Endurance and Conditioning

## Principles

1. **Team sports are intermittent.** Conditioning must reflect repeated short
   high-intensity efforts with incomplete recovery — not continuous laps.
2. **The ball is the best conditioning tool for juniors.** Small-sided games (SSGs)
   produce equal or better aerobic adaptation than running drills in youth players,
   with simultaneous technical development and far higher engagement.
3. **Aerobic trainability peaks around PHV** — this is the stage to deliberately
   build the engine.
4. Children are natural interval athletes; they recover quickly from short bursts
   but tolerate long continuous work poorly (physically and motivationally).

## Small-Sided Game Conditioning Matrix

| Format | Pitch size guide | Dominant stimulus |
|---|---|---|
| 2v2 – 3v3 | small | Repeated sprint, agility, anaerobic |
| 4v4 – 5v5 | medium | High-intensity aerobic (sweet spot) |
| 7v7 – 9v9 | large | Aerobic volume, tactical positioning |

Dose for aerobic development: 3–4 blocks of 3–4 min (4v4/5v5), 90 s active recovery,
coach enforces high tempo (instant restarts, multiple balls ready, touch limits).

## Running-Based Conditioning (use sparingly, mainly post-PHV)

- **15/15 intervals:** 15 s run at ~MAS / 15 s walk × 8–12 min.
- **Repeated sprint ability:** 6–10 × 30 m, 20–30 s recovery, 2 sets (post-PHV).
- **Tempo runs:** 6–10 × 100 m at ~70%, walk-back recovery — useful low-cost volume.
- Pre-PHV: replace all of the above with games, relays and obstacle circuits.

## Rugby-Specific Conditioning Note

Conditioning must include get-up demands: efforts beginning from the ground
(down-up shuttles, tackle-bag hit → sprint → realign) reflect ruck-to-ruck demands.
Reserve full versions for contact-trained, post-PHV players; juniors use pad-free
down-ups.

## Football-Specific Conditioning Note

Position-aware from ~15+: wide players bias longer repeated sprints (30–40 m);
central players bias shorter, denser efforts with turns.

## Monitoring

- Yo-Yo Intermittent Recovery Test Level 1 (13+) or 30-15 IFT every 8–12 weeks.
- Simple RPE (1–10) after sessions; weekly sum gives a basic load number —
  avoid week-on-week jumps >20–30%.
