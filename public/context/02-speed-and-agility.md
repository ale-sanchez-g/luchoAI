# Speed and Agility for Youth Team-Sport Athletes

## Principles

1. **Speed is a skill.** Quality over quantity: short efforts (<6 s), full recovery
   (work:rest 1:5 to 1:8), stop the drill when technique degrades.
2. **Place speed work first** in the session, after warm-up, while the nervous system
   is fresh. Never run speed work on fatigued legs.
3. **Closed → open progression.** Master pre-planned movement patterns (cone drills)
   before reactive/chaotic agility (mirror games, evasion tag, small-sided games).
4. **Total high-intensity sprint volume:** pre-PHV ≈ 100–200 m per session;
   post-PHV ≈ 200–400 m per session.

## Core Session Template (60 min — matches "Explosive Sprinting and Agility" format)

| Block | Time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | 12 min | Dynamic warm-up + sprint drills (A-skips, high knees, wall drives) |
| Acceleration | 10 min | Hill sprints or resisted starts |
| Lateral speed | 10 min | Lateral shuttle runs |
| Agility | 20 min | Cone weaves → reactive evasion games |
| Cool-down | 8 min | Easy jog, mobility, reflection questions |

## Drill Library

### Hill Sprints (acceleration)
- 20 m uphill at maximum effort, walk-back recovery, 3 sets of 3–4 reps.
- Cues: explosive first three steps; stay low; punch the ground back; arms drive
  cheek-to-cheek.
- Age scaling: pre-PHV use 10–15 m and gentler gradient; post-PHV up to 30 m.
- No hill available: resisted partner-band starts or sled pushes (post-PHV only).

### Lateral Shuttle Runs (change of direction)
- Two markers 10 m apart; shuttle side-to-side focusing on plant-and-push mechanics.
- Cues: feet light and fast; small steps into the turn; push off the outside leg;
  eyes up, not at feet.
- Progression: 5 m (younger) → 10 m → add a ball pass on each turn → add a reactive
  caller who changes direction mid-rep.

### Cone Weave / Zig-Zag (agility)
- 6–8 cones in a zig-zag, 2–3 m apart; weave at controlled speed building to maximal.
- Cues: stay low and balanced through cuts; eyes up to the next cone; decelerate
  before the cut, accelerate out of it.
- Progression: walk-through → ¾ speed → full speed → carry a ball → race format →
  defender shadow.

### Acceleration Ladders (flat-ground starts)
- 5 m, 10 m, 15 m, 20 m sprints from varied starts (standing, lying, half-kneel,
  facing backwards). 2 reps each, full recovery.
- Excellent for football/rugby because match sprints start from chaos, not blocks.

### Reactive Agility Games (open skill — the transfer layer)
- **Mirror drill:** attacker tries to lose the defender across a 5 m channel, 5 s bouts.
- **Coloured-cone calls:** coach calls colour, athlete sprints to it; add fakes.
- **Evasion tag / British bulldog variants:** the highest-transfer agility work for
  pre-PHV athletes — keep these in every junior session.
- **Rondo-to-sprint (football) / offload-to-support line (rugby):** technical action
  immediately followed by a maximal 10 m effort.

## Maturation Adjustments

- **Pre-PHV:** bias frequency and coordination (fast feet, rhythm, tag games);
  plyometric content low-level only (skips, hops, low bounds).
- **Circa-PHV:** cut sprint volume ~30%; re-coach mechanics; expect temporary
  clumsiness and protect confidence.
- **Post-PHV:** add force-oriented methods — resisted sprints, sled work, and
  combine with the strength program in ytkb-003 for best transfer.

## Simple Monitoring

- 10 m and 20 m sprint times, 505 change-of-direction test, every 8–12 weeks.
- Track against the athlete's own history only — never rank publicly at junior level.
